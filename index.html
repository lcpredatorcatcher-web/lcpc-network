The Method

How a catch works

It started in 2020, after older men messaged Musa's then-16-year-old daughter. He has run it the same way since — one-man operation, everything documented, nothing hidden.

STEP 01

The chats

Posing as a 14-to-16-year-old online, Musa lets the other man set every term. The age is stated up front, in the source's exact words — and they keep writing anyway. Every message is timestamped and saved.

STEP 02

The meet

They pick the place — a Turkey Hill, a parking lot, a street corner. Musa is waiting with the camera rolling. The confrontation is the record: their words, their story changing in real time.

STEP 03

The handoff

Full transcripts go to police. Luzerne County prosecutes off this evidence — roughly 20 men charged. The video stays public, because a courtroom is private and a search engine is forever.

“Predators are more afraid of getting caught by me than of getting locked up. Jail is private. Exposure is forever — the job knows, the family knows, the whole town knows.”

— MUSA M. HARRIS, LC PREDATOR CATCHER

Project Safety Eye

County by County

A weekly series walking Pennsylvania's Megan's Law registry, one county at a time — every name, town, and tier read straight from the official registry, so you can check your own street. New episode every Friday. Episode 1: Lackawanna County — now live. Watch it on YouTube → Next county: announced soon — vote in the comments on the LC Predator Catcher page.

How every episode is built

  • Registry first. The count comes from the official Megan's Law pull — and one unchecked box on that search can hide dozens of names, including people incarcerated out of county. Every episode shows you the box.
  • Faces included. Convicted men on the registry get full face cards — name, town, tier, charges, spoken out loud. Convicted men missing from the registry get a card too, stamped with why: locked up elsewhere, pled down, never registered.
  • Compliance flags shown. If the registry flags a man for failure to register or bad information, the card says so — locals recognize faces, and real tips go to police, never the comments.
  • No addresses, ever. Names, towns, and tiers are public record. Street addresses and whereabouts posted anywhere get removed — that's the line, and it doesn't move.

Footage Licensing

Read before you email

Networks, shows, and documentaries: LCPC footage isn't free clips. These are the terms — plain and simple — for using any catch footage.

  1. 01Paid upfront. No free clips, no “exposure” deals. A single-segment license starts around $500–$1,000 per clip — more for exclusive or documentary rights.
  2. 02Credit on screen. Every use carries the on-screen credit “Footage: LC Predator Catcher,” plus a link to the channel in the video description.
  3. 03One use, one segment. You're renting the footage, not buying it. One license covers one use in one segment — no re-use, no resale, no handing it off.
  4. 04Approval before air. Musa sees and approves the final cut before it airs. Nothing goes out without his sign-off.
  5. 05No hit pieces. Footage can't be edited or used to paint Musa as the villain. If the segment is built to make him look like the bad guy, the license doesn't happen.

Licensing inquiries: lcpredatorcatcher@gmail.com — put “Footage Licensing” in the subject line.
